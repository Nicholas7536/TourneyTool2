Handoff Document — Strikers Club “Steal-a-Player” Tournament PoC
1. Objective

Build a Python proof-of-concept simulator in Replit for a custom online soccer tournament format.

The simulator's purpose is to test whether the tournament rules produce:

Interesting roster growth/shrinkage
Lots of eliminations
Recovery from 2v2
Teams progressing 3 → 4 → 5
Multiple possible finalist teams
Reasonable tournament length
Edge cases/deadlocks
Whether 24 players is a good pool size
Whether the tournament can end with only 1 finalist or preferably 2

The game being modeled is an online soccer game similar to Strikers Club.

Hard game constraint

The custom lobby supports:

Minimum: 4 players = 2v2
Maximum: 10 players = 5v5

Therefore:

2v2, 3v3, 4v4, and 5v5 are possible.

No 6v6 should be used in the PoC.

2. Core Concept

Everyone starts at 3 players per team.

The tournament is a roster-growth ladder.

A team's size determines its level:

Level	Roster	Match
Level 0	2	2v2
Level 1	3	3v3
Level 2	4	4v4
Level 3	5	5v5 / finalist

The fundamental mechanic is:

Winner STEALS exactly 1 player from the losing team.

Never borrow.

Never temporarily loan.

The stolen player permanently joins the winner.

The losing team therefore loses one player.

3. Match Transitions

These are the fundamental transitions.

3v3
3 + 3
  ↓
WINNER = 4
LOSER  = 2


So:

3 + 3 → 4 + 2

4v4
4 + 4
  ↓
WINNER = 5
LOSER  = 3


So:

4 + 4 → 5 + 3

The 5-player team becomes a finalist and leaves the active ladder.

2v2
2 + 2
  ↓
WINNER = 3
LOSER  = 1


The remaining 1 player has no team and is:

ELIMINATED.

So the active-player population decreases by 1.

4. Goalkeeper Rule

Original concept:

A player from each team starts as goalkeeper.
After every goal, both teams rotate their goalkeeper.

The match simulator does not necessarily need to model individual goalkeeper identities initially.

For the first PoC, treat goalkeeper rotation as a match-level rule/metadata item.

The important tournament simulation is roster size and stealing.

6. Team Matching Philosophy

The desired default rule is:

Teams normally play another team at the same level.

So:

2 vs 2 → 2v2
3 vs 3 → 3v3
4 vs 4 → 4v4


A team does not automatically play a larger/smaller team.

Waiting is intentional.

Example:

LEVEL 2
C = 4

LEVEL 1
G = 3
D = 3

LEVEL 0
H = 2


C should WAIT.

G and D play 3v3.

The resulting roster changes may eventually create another 4-player team for C to play.

This is an important part of the intended design.

7. Why Waiting Matters

The tournament is intended to behave like a pipeline:

2v2
 ↓
3v3
 ↓
4v4
 ↓
5v5
 ↓
FINALIST


Losses flow downward:

5
 ↓
4
 ↓
3
 ↓
2
 ↓
ELIMINATION


Thus Level 0 acts as a recovery/elimination area.

Example:

2 + 2
 ↓
3 + eliminated player


The 3-player winner returns to Level 1.

Then it can eventually reach 4 and 5.

This creates the desired comeback path:

2 → 3 → 4 → 5

8. Finalist Rule

Current preferred concept:

When a team reaches 5 players, it has "won" its portion of the tournament and becomes a finalist.

The team:

Locks its 5 players
Leaves the active ladder
Cannot continue stealing players
Waits while the rest of the tournament continues

Preferred final:

First 2 teams to reach 5 become the 2 finalists.

Then:

FINALIST A = 5
FINALIST B = 5

        ↓

🏆 5v5 GRAND FINAL


However:

Exactly 1 finalist is acceptable if everyone else is eventually eliminated before another team reaches 5.

This is an important outcome to test mathematically.

9. Theoretical Finalist Count With 24 Players

Starting population:

24

Maximum possible 5-player teams:

floor(24 / 5) = 4


So theoretically:

Maximum = 4 finalist teams

Minimum possible:

1 finalist

if the rest of the population is eliminated.

However, under the preferred tournament rule:

Stop the qualification phase as soon as the second team reaches 5.

Therefore the intended normal outcome is:

2 finalists.

10. Important Mathematical Fact About Eliminations

A Level 0 loss:

2 + 2
 ↓
3 + 1


causes 1 player to be permanently eliminated.

Therefore if 24 players eventually collapse to one 5-player finalist:

24 - 5 = 19


There must be:

19 eliminated players.

This makes the one-finalist scenario mathematically possible.

The simulator should test whether actual legal match sequences can produce it.

12. Potential Deadlock Solution

The current preferred concept is:

Normal rule

Only same-level teams play.

If no same-level opponent exists:

WAIT.

Emergency fallback

If the entire tournament becomes mathematically stuck, allow the nearest available levels to play.

Example:

D = 4
G = 2


Emergency match:

2v2

D has 4 players but only fields 2.

If D wins:

D: 4 → 5
G: 2 → eliminated


If G wins:

G: 2 → 3
D: 4 → 3


Now:

D = 3
G = 3


and normal 3v3 resumes.

Important

This fallback should NOT be the normal matchmaking system.

It exists specifically to prevent a deadlocked tournament.

14. Important Observation

If you simply pair same-level teams as quickly as possible, 24 players can reach multiple 5-player teams surprisingly quickly.

For example:

3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3
        ↓
4,4,4,4,2,2,2,2
        ↓
5,5,3,3,2,2,2,2


Therefore the PoC should test different matchmaking policies, not just one.

The purpose is to discover what produces the desired:

17. Match Result Simulation

Initially, don't worry about actual soccer scoring.

Use a probability model.

Simplest:

winner = random.choice([team_a, team_b])


Better later:

Give teams a hidden skill value.

For example:

skill = random.uniform(0.5, 1.5)


Then calculate win probability.

This lets the simulator test:

Purely random tournaments
Strong teams
Weak teams
Skill-balanced teams
Underdog comebacks
18. Tournament Loop

Conceptual algorithm:

while tournament_not_finished:

    check_for_finalists()

    if two_finalists_exist:
        break

    find_same_level_pairs()

    if pairs_exist:
        play_matches()
        resolve_steals()
        eliminate_level_zero_losers()
        continue

    if tournament_is_stuck:
        invoke_emergency_matching()

    if no_possible_match_exists:
        terminate_as_edge_case()

19. Match Resolution

For a normal same-level match:

winner.players += 1
loser.players -= 1


Then:

if loser.players == 1:
    loser.eliminated = True


And:

if winner.players == 5:
    winner.finalist = True
    winner.active = False


The actual identity of the stolen player should ideally be tracked, even if the first PoC only needs counts.

24. Matchmaking Policies to Test

At minimum, implement these three:

Policy A — Strict

Only same-level teams can play.

If no opponent:

WAIT.

This is closest to the current intended rules.

Policy B — Strict + Emergency

Same-level matching normally.

If the tournament becomes completely stuck:

nearest-level fallback.

This is currently the most promising ruleset.

Policy C — Immediate nearest-level

Always find the closest roster size.

This is probably less desirable, but useful as a comparison.

26. Important Conceptual Rule

The tournament is not a conventional bracket.

It is better thought of as a population ladder / ecosystem.

Teams are constantly moving:

       5 🏆
       ▲
       │
       4
       ▲
       │
       3
       ▲
       │
       2
       │
       ▼
      ☠


A team can therefore have a comeback like:

3 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5


This is one of the main reasons to build the simulator.

27. One Critical Rule Still Under Investigation

The PoC should not assume the emergency matching rule is final.

The main design question is:

What should happen if the only active teams are at different levels and neither has a same-level opponent?

Example:

4-player team
     +
2-player team


Strict same-level rules produce a deadlock.

Possible solution:

4-player team vs 2-player team
        ↓
      2v2


The 4-player team fields 2 players.

If it wins:

4 → 5
2 → eliminated


If the 2-player team wins:

2 → 3
4 → 3


The teams reunite at Level 1.

This is a promising emergency mechanism, but simulation should determine whether it creates good or bad tournament behavior.
